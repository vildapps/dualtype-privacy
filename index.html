Dualtype — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 September 2026

Dualtype is a browser extension that shows a translation of the message you are typing on WhatsApp Web, and keeps the phrases you send as a personal review deck. This policy describes exactly what the extension can see, what it does with it, and where it goes. It is short because the extension does very little.

The short version. There is no Dualtype server. We do not collect, receive, store, transmit or sell any of your data. We cannot see your messages, your phrase deck, or your settings, because none of it ever reaches us.

What the extension can access

Text you type into the WhatsApp Web message box, and only while the translation strip is turned on. The extension reads the message box on web.whatsapp.com so it can translate what you are writing.

The extension does not read your received messages, your chat history, your contacts, your phone number, your profile, or any other part of WhatsApp. It does not send messages, modify messages, or act on your account in any way.

The extension runs only on web.whatsapp.com. It has no access to any other website you visit.

Where that text goes

When you pause typing, the text in the message box is sent to the translation provider you have selected in settings, and to nowhere else. You choose the provider:

ProviderWhere your text goes
MyMemory (default)api.mymemory.translated.net — privacy policy
LibreTranslateThe server you configure. If you point it at your own machine, the text never leaves your computer.
DeepLapi.deepl.com — privacy policy
Claude (Anthropic)api.anthropic.com — privacy policy
Google Cloud Translationtranslation.googleapis.com — privacy policy

Your text is handled by that provider under their privacy policy, not ours. If you would rather no text left your machine at all, run a local LibreTranslate instance and select it in settings.

Text is sent only when you have typed at least two characters and then paused. Messages longer than 1,200 characters are not sent at all. Repeated phrases are served from a local in-memory cache and are not re-sent.

If your chosen provider fails, the extension shows you an error. It does not reroute your message to a different service. There is an off-by-default setting that permits falling back to MyMemory when a provider fails; if you turn it on, the strip tells you each time it happens. A LibreTranslate server of your own never falls back, whatever that setting says.

The optional contact email. MyMemory offers a higher free daily limit to requests that carry an email address. If — and only if — you enter one in settings, it is included in requests to MyMemory. Leave that box empty and no email is ever sent.

What is stored, and where

Everything the extension stores lives in your browser's local extension storage (chrome.storage.local) on this computer:

  • Your settings — language pair, chosen provider, timing preferences.
  • Your API keys, if you entered any. These are stored locally and are transmitted only to the provider they belong to, in order to authenticate your own requests.
  • Your phrase deck — the phrases you sent, their translations, and their review schedule.

This data is not synced to any account, not backed up anywhere, and not readable by us or by anyone else. It stays in this browser profile.

What is not done

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage tracking.
  • No advertising, and no data sold or shared with data brokers.
  • No user accounts, no sign-in, no identifiers of any kind.
  • No remote code execution. The extension ships all of its code in the package.
  • Nothing is written to logs.

Permissions, and why each one exists

  • storage — to save your settings and your phrase deck on this computer.
  • Host access to web.whatsapp.com — to read the message box and draw the translation strip above it.
  • Host access to the translation API hosts listed above — so the extension's background worker can request a translation from the provider you selected. WhatsApp's content security policy blocks these requests from the page itself, so they are made from the extension's own background context.
  • Host access to localhost and 127.0.0.1 — so you can point the extension at a LibreTranslate instance running on your own machine.

No other permissions are requested. The extension does not request access to browsing history, tabs, cookies, downloads, your clipboard, or any site other than those listed.

Deleting your data

  • Phrase deck: open the deck page and click Clear deck. You can export it to CSV first.
  • Everything else: removing the extension from chrome://extensions deletes all of its stored data, including your API keys.

Because none of it ever left your computer, there is nothing for us to delete on our side.

Children

Dualtype is not directed at children under 13 and collects no information from anyone.
